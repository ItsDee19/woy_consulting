The 4D approach

A structured path from diagnosis to sustained adoption.

Rapid deep dives into the real challenges through conversations with the people closest to them. We test the assumptions the organisation has stopped noticing, and surface the gaps and opportunities that the current view is missing.

Sharpen the problem, set the outcomes, and prioritise. This is also where we agree what to drop. A clear list of what the organisation is not doing is usually worth more than another initiative.

Co-create the journey, the deliverables, the dependencies and the timelines. Proven frameworks are adapted to your operating reality, ownership model and maturity stage, so teams can actually run them.

We support implementation, refine in real time, and build internal capability so the change sustains without us. Success metrics, operating cadences and accountability loops stay behind when we leave.

Case studies

Client names are withheld under confidentiality agreements. Industry and scope of work are shown.

Strengthening senior sales leadership

A senior sales leadership group needed development that connected individual growth to the business context they were being measured on.

What we did

  • Leadership conversations, participant surveys and team climate feedback to establish a shared starting point.
  • Personalised development plans with specific behavioural goals for each leader.
  • One to one coaching, leadership workshops and team coaching applied to live challenges.

What changed

  • Greater willingness across the cohort to own individual development areas.
  • Stronger peer relationships, observed and confirmed by the CHRO.
  • Leaders shifted focus from daily execution to scalable governance and long-term impact.

Strategy, governance, culture and people systems

An education institution running two curriculum contexts needed one growth agenda, and the operating discipline to deliver it.

What we did

  • Consolidated five, three and one year roadmaps with initiatives, KPIs, owners and dependencies.
  • Service mapping, delegation of authority, approval routes and functional risk matrices.
  • Values translated into observable behaviours and cascaded to leadership, administration and teaching staff.

What changed

  • A single growth agenda shared across academic and support functions.
  • Defined service commitments, approval authorities and review routines.
  • Role-specific goal sheets, rating guidance and calibration built into the performance cycle.

Consultative selling mastery

Account managers were selling transactionally in a market that rewarded trusted advisor relationships.

What we did

  • Translated consulting principles into a sales capability framework built for the sector.
  • Simulation-led learning mapped to real client contexts and live accounts.
  • Embedded tools and behaviours so skills transferred immediately rather than after the workshop.

What changed

  • Clients began treating account managers as strategic partners.
  • Rework fell through stronger discovery and clearer contracting.
  • Teams adopted a shared consultative language across functions.

Entrepreneurial mindset for financial services leaders

Capable managers were operating risk-averse in a business that needed opportunity recognition and faster decisions.

What we did

  • Structured the programme around entrepreneurship competence, empathy, business acumen, risk and innovation.
  • Used sector case studies, empathy mapping, prototyping and financial analysis labs.
  • Linked leadership behaviour directly to P&L drivers and investment choices.

What changed

  • Participants moved from risk-averse management toward entrepreneurial thinking.
  • Sharper customer insight translated into practical prototypes.
  • Better decisions on cost trade-offs and return on investment.

Leadership assimilation for a unified technical centre

A newly unified technical centre carried several inherited identities and needed to operate as one organisation.

What we did

  • Mapped systemic and behavioural blockers, moving the group from blame toward ownership.
  • Co-created leadership principles, shared governance and role modelling.
  • Ran cross-functional case jams on live AI and agile delivery challenges.

What changed

  • Leaders aligned on why the transformation mattered, and why now.
  • A stronger collective identity across previously separate groups.
  • A co-owned charter of commitments with individual pledges attached.

Strategic thinking and global perspective

High-potential women leaders were delivering operational excellence but operating below enterprise-level influence.

What we did

  • Designed a one-day intensive inside a six-month leadership journey.
  • Reframed roles from execution to enterprise contribution through simulations.
  • Built stakeholder mapping, leadership identity work and specific action commitments.

What changed

  • Participants reframed their contribution from operational to enterprise level.
  • Clearer recognition of cross-geography and cross-function dependencies.
  • Each leader left with one specific, observable strategic action.
